## Hostile condition

The two worlds had exactly equal initial observations and equal evidence affordances:

```text
adequacy_A_within_model_uncertainty
adequacy_B_model_inadequate
```

Available evidence in both worlds:

```text
ordinary_discriminator
model_disrupting_probe
```

The `ordinary_discriminator` was verified to remain observationally non-discriminating across the pair. The `model_disrupting_probe` was verified to expose no residual in the within-model world and a causal-vocabulary residual in the model-inadequate world.

## Primary comparison

| Policy | Non-guessing initial action | Requested model-adequacy probe | Correct after probe: within-model | Correct after probe: model inadequate | Full pass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| causal negative-space search | yes | **yes** | yes | yes | **yes** |
| general causal reasoner | yes | **no** | yes | yes | no |

The strong causal reasoner requested additional evidence inside the current causal model. When the model-disrupting evidence was supplied anyway, it interpreted both regimes correctly.

Therefore the observed difference is localized to:

```text
evidence acquisition / investigative attention
```

not:

```text
post-evidence causal interpretation
```

This preserves the intended separation:

```text
Psi != U
```

## Other controls

No other initial baseline produced a full pass. Gap and self-confirming opportunity search also failed the non-guessing criterion. Novelty, performance, and random controls did not select the model-adequacy probe and did not correctly classify both post-probe regimes.

## Evidential update

This run supports the narrow implementation claim:

> After repeated ordinary causal probes remain inconclusive, the tested explicit negative-space policy can redirect investigative attention toward testing whether the causal vocabulary itself is adequate, while the tested strong general causal baseline continues evidence acquisition within the existing model.

It does **not** establish:

- general superiority of negative-space search over strong causal reasoning;
- that general causal reasoners cannot learn the same escalation policy;
- transfer to different surface signatures or unseen causal structures;
- that the trigger `ordinary_hypotheses_unresolved` is itself discovered rather than supplied;
- real-world model-inadequacy detection.

The benchmark is intentionally aligned with the hypothesized mechanism. A stronger next test must vary or hide the escalation signature so that a policy cannot succeed by special-casing this exact history pattern.

## Relationship to prior negative results

### NR-001 remains open

v0.1.1 demonstrates a condition under which the explicit policy selects and interprets a model-adequacy probe. It does not repair the original `E_model_inadequate` failure, whose initial observation still does not trigger correct localization or evidence selection.

### NR-002 remains valid but narrowed

v0.1 showed no incremental advantage over strong causal reasoning on ordinary observational equivalence. v0.1.1 produces a difference on the new evidence-selection task, but only under this controlled escalation signature. NR-002 must not be rewritten as if the earlier null disappeared.

## Next discriminator

Create held-out model-adequacy environments in which:

- the number and type of prior failed ordinary probes vary;
- the surface capability and payoff fields vary;
- some repeated failures are caused by noise rather than model inadequacy;
- the model-disrupting probe has a cost;
- the strong causal baseline has access to the same history and can adapt its search policy.

The next claim should be about transfer of the escalation rule, not success on this hand-constructed pair.
