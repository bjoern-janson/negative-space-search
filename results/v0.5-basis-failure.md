## Specificity result

Both interaction held-outs were solved correctly after composition:

```text
H_INT_1 -> interaction_probe
H_INT_2 -> interaction_probe
```

The constituent-only controls were also preserved:

```text
H_DEP_ONLY -> independence_probe
H_PAY_ONLY -> payoff_regime_probe
```

Thus the constructed relation improved specificity rather than merely increasing interaction recall.

A useful nuance is that the fixed single-basis selector already recognized both positive interaction cases. Its held-out failure occurred on `H_DEP_ONLY`: `pair0_close` activated both the independence and interaction detectors, producing an unnecessary `model_disrupting_probe` decision. The gain from composition is therefore not simply detecting interaction positives. It is preventing an overbroad constituent from contaminating another valid class.

## Novel case

All learned systems routed:

This remains a fixed no-match policy and does not establish invention of a new relation operator.

## Evidential update

v0.5 supports the narrow claim:

> In this controlled benchmark, a learner can detect that its supplied single-relation basis is inadequate for one evidence-selection class and conditionally construct a useful conjunction from supplied primitive relations. The repaired basis improves held-out evidence selection while avoiding the search cost of indiscriminate composition across already-adequate probe families.

This moves the demonstrated capability from:

```text
select a useful supplied relation
```

in v0.4 to:

```text
detect single-basis insufficiency
-> construct a composite relation
-> preserve it only where it improves transfer
```

## What v0.5 does not establish

Most importantly:

```text
basis composition != primitive operator invention
```

The learner was explicitly supplied with:

- the raw paired-measurement interface;
- the primitive relation operators from v0.4;
- the `AND` composition operator;
- the adequacy threshold;
- the maximum composition depth of one;
- the candidate-scoring rule;
- the no-match -> `model_disrupting_probe` policy.

Therefore v0.5 does not establish:

- autonomous invention of `AND`, OR, XOR, negation, arithmetic, or other relation constructors;
- discovery of which raw measurements should be paired;
- unrestricted interface invention;
- robustness to corrupted resolving-probe labels;
- robustness to large or open-ended construction languages;
- superiority over generic program synthesis or learned representation methods;
- unique causal value of negative-space framing.

v0.5 contains numeric jitter and misleading surface hints, but **not label noise**. Do not describe it as label-noise robustness.

## Relationship to v0.4

v0.4 showed that a learner could abandon previously rewarded surface relations and select better relations from a supplied candidate library.

v0.5 removes one more easy explanation: the exact useful relation for `interaction_probe` is absent from the initial library. The learner must first localize that the single basis is inadequate and then construct a conjunction.

However, the constructor itself remains supplied.

## Next discriminator

The next test should attack the construction language rather than merely add more conjunction cases.

A useful v0.6 should include at least one failure whose correct distinction cannot be represented by any supplied single relation or pairwise `AND` composition. Candidate directions include:

1. withhold the required composition operator while preserving more primitive numeric operations;
2. include adequate controls so construction-language expansion is not triggered globally;
3. charge explicitly for operator search and representation complexity;
4. include corrupted or ambiguously attributed resolving outcomes;
5. compare a gated constructor against a generic program-synthesis baseline with identical primitives and budget;
6. preserve the null if generic synthesis recovers the same operator at equal or lower cost.

The next question is:

> Can the system identify that its **construction language** is inadequate and generate a new relation operator from more primitive operations, rather than merely composing within a supplied language?
