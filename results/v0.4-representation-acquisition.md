## Compressed-memory failure

After all nine resolved histories were available, compressed prediction/outcome/error retrieval selected:

```text
R_DEP   -> B_N  -> topology_probe       wrong
R_PAY   -> B_D  -> independence_probe   wrong
R_NET   -> B_P  -> payoff_regime_probe  wrong
R_NOVEL -> A_P1 -> payoff_regime_probe  wrong
```

Its held-out rate fell from `0.50` before the repair histories to `0.00` after them.

This is a useful local warning: adding more resolved cases does not guarantee better future search when the persistent representation preserves misleading similarity rather than transferable structure.

## Evidential update

v0.4 supports the narrow claim:

> In this synthetic benchmark, a learner can revise a previously rewarded but non-transferable representation after diagnostic failures, using raw resolved histories and a frozen generic relation library, and that representation repair can improve later evidence selection.

The observed improvement is not merely case memorization: the learner exposes a complete change in its selected representation, and the repaired distinctions transfer to held-out cases whose surface cues and prediction/outcome similarity are misleading.

## Cost update

The repair was not free:

```text
representation cost: 3 -> 6
```

The original surface representation was cheaper. The repaired representation earned its additional complexity only after the surface abstraction failed.

Future benchmarks should therefore treat representation cost as part of the comparison rather than allowing richer structure to expand without penalty.

## What v0.4 does not establish

Most importantly, v0.4 does **not** demonstrate unrestricted interface invention.

The learner was given a frozen generic candidate library containing the relation operators needed for the solution:

```text
high / low / near_zero
close / far / sign_disagree
```

It learned which relations to preserve and which probes they predicted; it did not invent those operators.

v0.4 also does not establish:

- a uniquely negative-space mechanism;
- that the paired raw measurement interface is itself discovered;
- robustness to noisy or incorrect resolving-probe outcomes;
- robustness to irrelevant candidate relations at larger scale;
- transfer to unseen relation operators;
- superiority over learned representation methods;
- real-world discovery performance.

The result should therefore be described as **representation selection and repair**, not full representation invention.

## Relationship to v0.3

v0.3 showed:

```text
typed representation > compressed history
```

while finding no difference between typed general causal and negative-space-labelled histories.

v0.4 removes one part of the supplied-representation objection. The useful typed distinctions are no longer handed to the adaptive learner as causal labels; they are selected from raw resolved episodes after a cheaper surface abstraction fails.

But the generic relation operators remain supplied. The next boundary is therefore one level deeper.

## Next discriminator

Do not add more semantic fields to the representation.

The next experiment should attack the candidate relation library itself:

1. add many irrelevant candidate operators and charge for search/representation complexity;
2. make some resolving-probe outcomes noisy or incorrectly attributed;
3. withhold at least one relation operator required by a held-out failure;
4. allow a generic learner to construct or compose candidate relations from more primitive raw operations;
5. test whether failure of the current relation basis causes a localized representation revision rather than arbitrary feature expansion;
6. preserve a null if generic representation learning matches any negative-space-guided process.

The next question is:

> Can the system repair not only which distinction it trusts, but the basis from which candidate distinctions can be constructed?
